What it can do today

The Assistant is not small. Its weakness is that the abilities stop at operations instead of finishing recognizable household jobs.

Conversation

  • Keep per-user history and retain an in-flight turn or draft across navigation.
  • Stream, stop, retry interrupted turns, and fall back once after provider failure.
  • Reply in English or Dutch while keeping recipe ingredient lookup names Dutch.
  • Read up to two transient photos without storing them in chat history.
  • Use an explicit shared household profile, configurable models, and daily spend caps.
  • Show plans, compact tool activity, confirmations, proposals, links, and undo receipts.

Stock & freezer

  • Search freezer/pantry by category, age, expiry, storage section, and sort.
  • Add, merge, remove, update, and review 2–10 atomic inventory changes.
  • Classify stock, flag uncertainty, link leftovers, and set staple/freezer targets.
  • Explain inventory history, attribute actors, and undo safe single or batch changes.

Plans & meals

  • Read weeks, add/remove meals, mark cooked, and log a rating or note.
  • Suggest meals from stock overlap, stale freezer items, repeats, recent history, and frozen portions.
  • Distinguish fresh cooking from serving freezer stock and derive only fresh-side shopping needs.
  • Respect configurable repeat windows, suggestion counts, servings, and recipe yields.

Recipes

  • Find/search recipes; save from text, dictation, photo, screenshot, or URL.
  • Combine existing recipes into one live meal composition.
  • Stage typed corrections and current AH product-form choices for selective review.
  • Set stable ingredient roles and maintain translation/cook-mode background caches.

Shopping & AH

  • Derive a week list from planned recipe ingredients minus stock.
  • Preserve source provenance, optional ingredients, substitutions, and fresh-side rules.
  • Search bounded live AH evidence with Dutch queries, package size, price, and current product forms.
  • The app can preview/push to AH, but chat cannot yet control the ordinary list or final push.

Trust

  • Require an exact current read before persistent writes and reject stale targets.
  • Latch later writes after a contract/provenance failure.
  • Confirm destructive inventory changes and review recipe content before apply.
  • Claim completion only after a committed tool result; keep external AH search read-only.
The first obvious seam: a person can say “add milk to the list,” but the Assistant has no ordinary Shopping read/write tools. Similar seams exist for moving a meal, starting a timer, finishing the cooking bookkeeping, and noticing what needs attention.

From tools to service

The roadmap is organized as four completed loops over one trust contract. This is the difference between “the agent called a tool” and “the household job is done.”

01
Stock → Shopping

“We are out,” “used the last,” “bought this.”

→

Keep stock and the one Shopping list in agreement, with one reviewed outcome and receipt.

02
Plan → Shop

“Sort next week and get us ready.”

→

Compare meals, edit days/servings, reconcile the list, resolve conflicts, preview AH, then confirm push.

03
Cook → Close

“We cooked it; two went in the freezer.”

→

Timers, cooked state, rating, stock use, frozen portions, and Shopping all finish together.

04
Notice → Decide

“What should I deal with?”

→

A bounded zero-spend brief explains a few useful cues without spamming chat or notifications.

The Butler contract

1Notice locallySQLite facts and deterministic rules first; no model spend on home load.
2Explain whyEvidence, why now, confidence, and missing information stay visible.
3RecommendOne assertive default plus alternatives and “not now.”
4BundleFinish related domains together instead of leaving remembered follow-ups.
5GateConsequence decides review. External and destructive work always stops.
6Learn explicitlySave scoped preferences only after a visible choice with edit/forget.

Shape the first slice

Recommendations are preselected. Changing the first loop also retags its ideas as “First slice”; every idea remains individually editable below.

1. Which loop should be built first?

The shared capability/action foundation is always first. This chooses the first complete user outcome.

2. How proactive at first?

Initiative is per domain, but the first default still establishes the product’s personality.

3. Include fridge in the first wave?

A fridge completes intuitive food/expiry language but is an honest R3 schema sweep.

4. How may it learn preferences?

This decides whether convenience remains explainable.

5. May anything auto-apply initially?

“Act” is deliberately separate from being proactive.

6. Where should proactive help live?

The floating/contextual Assistant was deliberately retired.

7. Proactive notifications now?

Push creates real attention cost and needs durable staleness semantics.

Recommended first delivery

Capability registry + action bundle, then a complete Plan → Shop loop.

  • Compare and select a week.
  • Edit days, servings, and fresh/freezer source.
  • Reconcile one Shopping list.
  • Resolve source/product conflicts.
  • Preview AH and confirm the exact push.

Opening the app spends nothing. No automatic write. No notification. No silent preference. No second Assistant entry point.
