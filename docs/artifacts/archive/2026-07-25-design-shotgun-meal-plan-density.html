Meal plan · Design shotgun · 25 July 2026

Less chrome. More actual planning.

Three ways to make the meal plan calmer while preserving exact portions, batch shortcuts, fresh/freezer choices, and week-specific shopping.

Fixed in every direction

  • No visible ×1 button; no selected multiplier means the recipe baseline.
  • ×2, ×3, and ×4 sit beside portions and toggle back to baseline.
  • Remove sits beside the meal title instead of owning a full-height column.
  • Page-level Shopping and Settings are treated as duplicates.
3

control bands per linked meal

Portions, batch multipliers, and source choices stack on phones before the next meal begins.

4

batch buttons where three suffice

×1 restates the default and is the difference between wrapping and fitting beside portions.

2×

page-level navigation duplicates

Shopping and Settings are already persistent destinations; each week also owns its shopping context.

Direction A · Recommended

Quiet week cards

Keep the familiar multi-week page, but strip duplicate page actions and compress each week into one clean identity-and-action header.

Strength
Biggest improvement with the least relearning.
Trade-off
Future week cards and their headers still repeat.
Optimizes for
Quick weekly scanning on a phone.

Comparison

What changes, and what it costs

Every direction includes the requested meal-row fix. The real choice is how aggressively to simplify the repeated page and week headers.

DirectionHeader calmMulti-week scanChange costMain risk
A · Quiet week cardsHighPreservedLowRepeated empty week cards remain.
B · One-week focusHighestHidden behind Previous and NextMediumHouseholds may lose useful cross-week awareness.
C · Week railHighFast, but rail-basedMediumHorizontal navigation and flatter rows may feel less obvious.

Decision

Pick the planning shape.

A is preselected because it removes the clearest duplication without changing how nearby weeks are scanned. Override it if the visual evidence makes a stronger case for focus or density.
